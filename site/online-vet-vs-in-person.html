Online vet vs in-person vet: when a video visit is enough and when you must go to a clinic

Quick answer: book an online vet for quick answers, after-hours questions, follow-up care, chronic-condition management, preventive care and medication questions. Go in person immediately - before any video call - if your pet has difficulty breathing, uncontrolled bleeding, collapses or seizes, has severe or sudden pain, suspected poisoning, major trauma, or is unable to urinate. Those seven signs are Vetster's own list, read from its online vet guide on 2026-09-26. A prescription is a separate question: it needs a valid vet-client-patient relationship, and whether that can be built over video depends on your state's rules.

The decision in one picture

Run the urgent list first, everything else second. Vetster's seven emergency signs come before any other consideration, and its preparation guide repeats the principle: many people use virtual care precisely to find out whether their pet needs in-person or emergency care.

Then split by what the vet can actually do through a screen. Symptoms, history, photos, movement on camera, medication questions and follow-ups travel well over video. Lab work, imaging, a hands-on exam and higher-risk prescribing do not - and Vetster's own two-column table draws that line for you.

The prescription question, which is where the confusion lives

Three separate layers decide whether you leave an online visit with medication. First the relationship: almost every state requires a valid vet-client-patient relationship before any vet can prescribe, and Vetster says the open question is whether that relationship can be established over video. Second the federal picture: for many prescription medications, federal guidance expects the relationship to rest on a real exam or visit. Third the judgment: Vetster's guide says prescribing is always the veterinarian's decision, and a vet may still ask for a hands-on exam, lab work or imaging first.

If the prescription does happen, the pharmacy is your choice - Vetster states it is not a pharmacy and sends the script directly to the local or online pharmacy you pick, with the service fee shown as a labelled line item before checkout.

Online vet vs in-person vet FAQ

Are online vets real veterinarians?

Yes. Vetster states that online vets are licensed veterinarians who have completed accredited veterinary training, and that they complete the same education and licensing process as all veterinarians.

Source: https://vetster.com/en/wellness/online-vet-visits-how-virtual-veterinary-care-works · checked 2026-09-26

When should I use an online vet instead of going in person?

For quick answers when something has changed with your pet, after hours when physical clinics are closed, follow-up care, chronic condition management, preventive recommendations, medication questions and quality-of-life discussions - plus the everyday clusters Vetster lists: skin, ear and eye concerns, digestive and urinary changes, behaviour, mobility and ongoing care.

Source: https://vetster.com/en/wellness/online-vet-visits-how-virtual-veterinary-care-works · checked 2026-09-26

When should I go straight to a clinic or emergency hospital?

Immediately, if your pet has difficulty breathing, uncontrolled bleeding, collapses or seizes, shows severe or sudden pain, is suspected of poisoning, has suffered major trauma, or is unable to urinate. Vetster also states plainly that telehealth is not a substitute for emergency care.

Source: https://vetster.com/en/wellness/online-vet-visits-how-virtual-veterinary-care-works · checked 2026-09-26

Can an online vet prescribe medication?

Only when a valid vet-client-patient relationship is in place and the condition is appropriate for virtual care. Whether that relationship can be built over video depends on state rules, federal guidance often expects a real exam first, and prescribing is always the veterinarian's decision. The prescription then goes to the pharmacy you choose.

Source: https://vetster.com/en/wellness/online-vet-prescriptions · checked 2026-09-26

What can an online vet not do?

They cannot perform a hands-on exam, and Vetster's own guidance puts conditions needing lab work or imaging, complex or serious illnesses, medications requiring close monitoring, higher-risk drugs and many controlled substances on the in-person side of the line.

Source: https://vetster.com/en/wellness/online-vet-prescriptions · checked 2026-09-26

Do I need my pet on camera during the appointment?

Yes, for as much of the appointment as possible. Vetster explains that the vet relies on what they can see on screen, that having your pet present creates the basis for the veterinary-client-patient relationship, and that without your pet on screen the vet may be unable to diagnose or prescribe.

Source: https://vetster.com/en/wellness/how-to-prepare-for-your-online-vet-appointment · checked 2026-09-26

Is there such a thing as a free online vet?

General pet information is widely available for free, but Vetster states that personalized care from a licensed veterinarian is typically a paid service, because licensed veterinarians are legally responsible for the care they provide.

Source: https://vetster.com/en/wellness/online-vet-visits-how-virtual-veterinary-care-works · checked 2026-09-26
