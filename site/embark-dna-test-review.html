Embark dog DNA test review: what the three kits report, what they cost and how long results take

Which kit to buy, in one look

Start from the question you actually have. If the question is 'what breed is my dog', Breed ID answers it at $109 and adds ancestry back to the great-grandparents plus Relative Finder. If the question is 'what should I watch for in my dog's health', the answer needs the health screen, and Embark's FAQ is explicit that this is the line between the two kits: Breed + Health adds 270+ genetic health conditions, traits and Allergy Risk Scores on top of everything Breed ID does.

The Age Test answers a different question entirely. It estimates calendar age and birthday from DNA methylation, works best for dogs past the puppy stage, and its page does not publish breed or health reporting - so it is a companion kit, not a substitute for either DNA test.

Timing and logistics are the same across the DNA kits: a 30-second cheek swab, activation online, a free pre-paid US mailer, and results in 2-4 weeks. Embark adds two constraints worth knowing before you buy - samples stay stable for 6 months, and the test itself expires 2 years after purchase.

What the results actually explain

The report is built in four layers, and only one of them is medical. Layer one is breed mix, ancestry and relatives - 400+ breeds with over 99% breed ancestry accuracy, a family tree back to the great-grandparents, and Relative Finder, where Embark says 94% of dogs tested find at least one match genetically related as human first cousins. Layer two is the health screen: 270+ genetic health conditions with plain-language explanations and next steps to bring to your vet. Layer three is traits - 55 physical traits covering size, coat, shedding and grooming needs. Layer four is the four Allergy Risk Scores on a 0-100% scale.

The medical layer is the one that carries weight, and Embark frames it as a conversation starter with your veterinarian rather than an answer. The company's own health page reports that 3 in 4 dogs tested have a carrier or at-risk result and that 1 in 3 dog parents change care after receiving results - which is exactly why the report arrives with an explanation and next steps rather than a verdict.

Embark dog DNA test - FAQ

Is the Embark dog DNA test worth it?

Embark's own answer is that a DNA test can uncover inherited health risks, explain certain traits and help you plan proactive care, especially when you share the results with your veterinarian - and that genetic health risks do not always show symptoms right away. Its health page adds that 3 in 4 dogs tested have a carrier or at-risk result and that 1 in 3 dog parents change care after receiving results. Those are Embark's figures, not an independent study.

Source: https://embarkvet.com/pages/how-it-works · checked 2026-09-26

Which Embark test should I buy?

Embark's FAQ splits it plainly: Breed ID covers breed mix, ancestry and relatives, while Breed + Health includes all of that plus screening for 270+ genetic health conditions, traits and Allergy Risk Scores. Embark also notes that genetic health screening and Allergy Risk Scores are exclusive to Breed + Health and Purebred tests, so a health read-out means choosing one of those two.

Source: https://embarkvet.com/products/breed-and-health-dog-dna-test-kit · checked 2026-09-26

How long do Embark results take?

Embark prints 2-4 weeks for Breed ID and Breed + Health once the lab has the sample, and its Age Test page prints 4-5 weeks - the How it works FAQ gives 4-6 weeks for Age Tests, so both published figures are shown here rather than averaged. You are notified by email when results are ready.

Source: https://embarkvet.com/pages/how-it-works · checked 2026-09-26

What does the health report actually explain?

Embark says you get 270+ genetic health results and 55 traits in one report, online or in the app, with plain-language explanations and the option to share it directly with your veterinarian. Its own examples on the page include MDR1 drug sensitivity, Mast Cell Tumor risk and von Willebrand Disease Type I, and Embark frames each result as an explanation plus next steps rather than a verdict.

Source: https://embarkvet.com/pages/health-insights · checked 2026-09-26

Can a DNA test replace my vet?

No, and Embark says so directly: a DNA test is not meant to replace your veterinarian, it works alongside your vet by giving you more information for clearer conversations about your dog's care. Embark also states that genetic testing is an assessment of risk, not a diagnosis.

Source: https://embarkvet.com/pages/how-it-works · checked 2026-09-26

What are Allergy Risk Scores?

Embark reports four scores - food, environmental, contact and flea allergy risk - on a 0-100% scale, and describes itself as the only dog DNA test screening genetic allergy risk. The page says the scores help owners and vets know which tests and treatments to focus on if symptoms develop; they ship with Breed + Health and Purebred tests.

Source: https://embarkvet.com/pages/dog-allergy-risk-scores-test · checked 2026-09-26

How does Relative Finder work, and will I find relatives?

Embark analyzes the sample against what it calls the world's largest canine genetic database and ranks every match by percentage of shared DNA, from immediate family to distant cousins. It states that 94% of dogs tested find at least one match genetically related as human first cousins, with optional messaging through the built-in chat.

Source: https://embarkvet.com/pages/relative-finder · checked 2026-09-26

How accurate is Embark's dog DNA test?

Embark advertises 99% breed identification accuracy and over 99% breed ancestry accuracy, powered by microarray-based testing on Illumina's CanineHD platform and a database it says covers over 3 million dogs. It also names Cornell University College of Veterinary Medicine as a research partner. These are the company's own accuracy claims.

Source: https://embarkvet.com/pages/science-and-research · checked 2026-09-26

What should I know before I swab?

Swab the inside of the cheek pouch and under the tongue for at least 30 seconds, activate the kit online and mail it back in the pre-paid envelope - shipping is free within the US. Embark notes samples stay stable for 6 months but tests expire 2 years after purchase, so plan the second test accordingly.

Source: https://embarkvet.com/products/breed-and-health-dog-dna-test-kit · checked 2026-09-26
