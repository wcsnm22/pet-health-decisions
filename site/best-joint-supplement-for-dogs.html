How this list is built

There is no independent test score for dog joint supplements that we can quote, and we will not invent one. So this list uses a narrower, honest rule: a product only appears if its own brand publishes the per-serving ingredient milligrams and the weight-based dosing table on an official page, because that is what you can actually verify before buying. Two brands pass that test today - Cosequin and Dasuquin, both from Nutramax - and this page reads only cosequin.com and dasuquin.com.

The selection criteria are not ours either. Cosequin's own buying guide says three things: talk to your veterinarian, review the ingredients, and check the manufacturing standards. Every pick below is scored against those three, and the numbers in the table are copied from the label pages with a link and a check date.

One thing this page deliberately leaves out: price. Neither official site publishes one - Cosequin ends each product in a SHOP HERE button, Dasuquin in a WHERE TO BUY link - so no price figure appears here rather than a marketplace number dressed up as an official one.

Reading the labels in one picture

The two brands sit in the same weight class, and the difference is what they add. Both start around 600 mg of glucosamine hydrochloride per soft chew for a small-to-medium dog, with chondroitin at 300 mg for Cosequin and 250 mg for Dasuquin. Dasuquin publishes a separate large-dog chew at 900 mg rather than telling you to feed more chews, while Cosequin keeps one chew and moves the chew count up the weight table.

The second line is where the formulas split. Cosequin spends it on MSM and omega-3s; Dasuquin spends it on ASU, Boswellia serrata and green tea extract. Neither is a verdict - it is just what the labels say, and your vet picks the one that fits your dog's history.

Best joint supplement for dogs - FAQ

What is the best joint supplement for dogs?

There is no independent ranking to quote, so this page picks from what the brands publish: Cosequin Soft Chews with MSM and Omega-3 for everyday use, Cosequin Chewable Tablets with MSM for dogs that prefer a tablet, Cosequin for Small Dogs for 5-25 lb dogs, Dasuquin Soft Chews for the ASU formula, Dasuquin Soft Chews for Senior Dogs and Dasuquin Advanced when your vet wants the veterinarian-exclusive formula. Cosequin's own guide says the decision starts with a visit to your veterinarian.

Source: https://www.cosequin.com/joint-supplement/for-dogs/about · checked 2026-09-26

How much glucosamine does my dog need?

Both brands dose by body weight rather than a single number. Cosequin's table gives 1 soft chew a day (600 mg glucosamine) for a 10-29 lb dog for the first 4-6 weeks, then 1 every other day; 2 a day then 1 for 30-59 lb; 3 a day then 1-2 for 60 lb and over. Dasuquin instead sells a small-to-medium formula at 600 mg per chew and a large formula at 900 mg per chew. Your veterinarian sets the final amount.

Source: https://www.cosequin.com/joint-supplement/dog/soft-chew/omega-3 · checked 2026-09-26

Do I have to ask my vet first?

Both brands say so in their own words. Cosequin's guide says to schedule a visit with your veterinarian before deciding which joint supplement to give your dog, because the vet will evaluate your dog and provide a recommendation for your pet's needs. Dasuquin's directions say to consult your veterinarian to determine which formula is right for your dog.

Source: https://www.cosequin.com/joint-supplement/for-dogs/about · checked 2026-09-26

Which ingredients should I look for?

Cosequin's guide tells buyers to research what is in the product and names high-quality ingredients like glucosamine and chondroitin sulfate as the ones that help support joint health. Both products carry those two; Cosequin adds MSM, omega-3s and (in the chewable) manganese, while Dasuquin adds avocado/soybean unsaponifiables, Boswellia serrata extract and green tea extract.

Source: https://www.cosequin.com/joint-supplement/for-dogs/about · checked 2026-09-26

How long does the label say to give it?

Both brands publish an initial loading period of 4-6 weeks followed by a maintenance level - for example Cosequin's soft chew drops from 2 a day to 1 a day for a 30-59 lb dog after the initial phase, and Dasuquin's senior table does the same. That is the brands' own instruction window; it is not a promise of results and not a substitute for your veterinarian's advice.

Source: https://www.cosequin.com/joint-supplement/dog/soft-chew/omega-3 · checked 2026-09-26

How much do these cost?

Cosequin's official site publishes no prices: the homepage lists products and a Where to Buy call to action instead of price figures, and each product page ends in a SHOP HERE button. Dasuquin's catalog does the same with a WHERE TO BUY link. This page therefore shows no prices rather than filling the gap with marketplace numbers.

Source: https://www.cosequin.com/ · checked 2026-09-26

Which one for a small dog or a senior dog?

For a small dog, Cosequin's omega-3 soft chew page says dogs under 10 lbs should use Cosequin for Small Dogs - a 45 count with 260 mg glucosamine per chew, dosed 1 a day for a 5-10 lb dog initially and 1 every other day after the first 4-6 weeks. For senior dogs both brands publish a dedicated formula: Cosequin for Senior Dogs adds beta-glucans, and Dasuquin for Senior Dogs adds alpha-lipoic acid plus 80 mg of omega-3s per chew.

Source: https://www.cosequin.com/joint-supplement/small-dog/soft-chew/omega-3 · checked 2026-09-26

What does this list check, and what does it not check?

It checks only what Cosequin and Dasuquin publish on their own pages - per-serving ingredient milligrams, weight-based dosing tables, quality-check counts, manufacturing claims, availability and the absence of prices - each with a source link and a check date of 2026-09-26. It does not run lab tests, does not rank the whole market (many brands publish no usable label data on their own sites) and does not quote marketplace prices.
